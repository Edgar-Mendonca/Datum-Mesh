## Vision (agreed 2026-10-31): read `docs/VISION.md`

**One open-source platform for structural and finite-element analysis, written by us, that proves its own answers.** Frames, plates and shells, solids, heat and vibration in one model; linear and nonlinear, static and dynamic, in one engine; small and simple however much it grows. Its scope: structural frame analysis (frames, plates, load cases, P-delta, dynamics; no design codes), nonlinear finite elements (large deflection, plasticity, rubber, contact) and, in time, general and explicit dynamics (steps, impact). It aims at a workflow an experienced analyst finds natural: model, steps, loads, solve, results, in that order.

- **One engine: a core and modules** (core: model, steps, sets, assembly, solvers, Newton/arc-length and time loops, results; modules: frames, plates, solids, materials, heat, contact; bench). Every analysis is a sequence of steps on one model. Modules plug in through a small fixed interface and carry their own benchmarks.
- **Our own solver**: mechanics written from published methods (Bathe's *Finite Element Procedures*, Simo and Hughes, Newmark/HHT, Crisfield); no porting of other programs' code. Outside code only as infrastructure (faer, METIS). GPL tools only as separate optional add-ons.
- **Order**: finish the frames-and-structures side on the new core first (0.4.0 core + P-delta/large-deflection frames, 0.4.1 plates and shells, 0.4.2 dynamics, 0.4.3 structural details), then nonlinear and dynamic solids (0.5–0.7).

## Purpose (agreed 2026-10-29; kept, and widened by the vision above)

To show any engineer or student how a part or structure carries load, heat and vibration (where it is stressed, how far it moves, how hot it gets) in a free app they can trust because it proves its own answers.

- **For:** mechanical, aerospace and process engineers checking parts (brackets, lugs, vessels, shafts, heat sinks); students and lecturers learning FEA; small firms without a commercial licence.
- **Different because:** trust you can see (benchmarks solved in the app, the equilibrium check, saying what was not verified); free and open (MIT, no licence server); simple (draw, load, solve, read); honest about its limits.
- **Frames stay in Datum Mesh** (2D and 3D frames and trusses, with frequencies and buckling): they carry the parts engineers check and teach FEA well. They do **not** grow into building design: no design-code checks or code load combinations.
- **Not:** a design-code checker, a CAD program, or a whole commercial suite on day one.
- **In the spirit of** (agreed 2026-10-30): the analysis side of a structural program for frames, and a general finite-element program for parts; not CAD, not a pre-processor for other solvers. Simple parts are drawn here (2D sketch tools, extrude, revolve); complex ones come from real CAD as STEP (0.4). Plan: `docs/PLAN-0.3-0.4.md`.
- **Leads to:** 3D parts (0.3: extruded and revolved, then any shape from CAD), beyond linear (0.4: yielding, large deflection, contact, through open solvers run as separate programs), heat and flow (0.5), validated 1.0 (validation report, user guide, signed installer).

## 1. Architecture

`crates/core` is the engine (pure Rust, no Tauri): the model and its edits, sections, and (from 0.1.1) elements, meshing, the solver, results and benchmarks. `src-tauri` is the shell: commands, files, options, recovery and the updater. `ui` is the window: it draws what the engine returns and sends edits as operations.

Units: the engine and the file use SI base units (m, N, Pa, K, W). The window converts for display in one place (`ui/src/units.ts`): SI shows mm, kN, MPa, °C; US shows in, kip, ksi, °F; frames show coordinates in m (ft), parts in mm (in).

### Release flow

`release.yml` (windows-latest): checks a pushed tag matches the version, takes the notes from CHANGELOG, runs the engine tests, builds the NSIS installer, writes `SHA256SUMS.txt` and publishes the GitHub Release. Run it from the Actions tab (tag pushes are blocked from Claude sessions).

### How we work

1. The user reviews a release and sends screenshots.
2. Reproduce first.
3. Big asks get a plan file and questions; build after the answers.
4. Engine first (with a test), then the UI.
5. Check every change in the real app (Xvfb screenshots, `tests_ui/realapp.sh`).
6. Add a UI test for every bug.
7. Run all checks: clippy `-D warnings`, `cargo test` (with the hardening tests and benchmarks), the UI type-check and UI tests, `cargo deny check`.
8. Release.
9. Report in plain language: what changed, what was checked, what is not done.

## 5. Known limits (0.6.0)

- Assemblies: parts are swept sketches (extrude, revolve), solid only (no shells or rigid surfaces yet); parts that touch are always tied (no contact or sliding until 0.6.2), and only where they touch within 2 % of a facet's size; ties by penalty (about 10⁻⁴ relative); large deformation of tied parts is refused; the tetrahedra are cut from prisms (the mesh follows the sweep, not a free mesher); one sketch plane per part (its own x-y), placed by three turns and a move; no sketching on a part's face yet; thermal stress on assemblies not yet.

## 5. Known limits (0.5.8)

- Large rotations in 3D frames: not with plates (those stay second order, P-delta); loads keep their directions (no follower loads; end moments are not semi-tangential, which matters only for moments about more than one axis at once); member loads become dead nodal loads with their fixed-end moments on each piece, as for plane frames; elastic only; small strains in each piece; the curve's "turn" is the angle of the node's total rotation vector, kept continuous past a half turn. Forces and tangent by finite differences: about 1e-9 relative accuracy, so very stiff or very large models converge to a looser balance.

## 5. Known limits (0.5.5)

- Imported meshes: tetrahedra only (no hexahedra or wedges from files), ASCII .msh 2.2/4.1 and .inp; one material per mesh; static, natural frequencies, steady heat (0.5.6), plasticity and large deformation (0.5.7); no thermal stress yet; up to 66 000 nodes; faces by a fixed 35° angle (no merging or splitting by hand yet); no STL (needs a mesher). Not yet tried on files from a real mesher.

## 5. Known limits (0.5.4)

- Datum Link: Mesh sends tables and values and reads materials; the other apps must adopt docs/LINKS.md before a sent packet opens there (until then the data is saved and copied). No pick-and-answer with Atlas yet (open the `.dlink` Atlas makes). Packets are found by the apps' per-user install folders.

## 5. Known limits (0.5.2)

- Large deformation: 3D solids only (not plane parts); neo-Hookean only (no Mooney–Rivlin, Ogden or plasticity at large strain); loads dead (pressure does not follow the face); no locking cure for nearly incompressible rubber beyond quadratic wedges (keep ν ≤ 0.49 or refine); load control, so no path past a limit point; load and support markers stay at the undeformed positions in the 3D view.

## 5. Known limits (0.5.1)

- 3D plasticity: swept solids only (wedges; no tetrahedra yet); small strains; load control to collapse as for parts; each tangent is factorised afresh, so large 3D models solve slowly (seconds per iteration past 20 000 unknowns); no thermal loads with yielding.

## 5. Known limits (0.5.0)

- Plasticity: plane and axisymmetric parts only (not 3D solids, frames or plates); small strains and small movements; J2 with linear isotropic hardening (no kinematic hardening, no other yield criteria); load control, so the collapse load is the largest load carried (a lower bound found to about 1/1000 of an increment), with no path past it; results per element (contours a little ragged at the plastic zone's edge); the safety factor is not meaningful once yielded.

## 5. Known limits (0.4.5)

- Member offsets: along the member's own axis only (no offsets across it); rigid zones are 10⁵ times stiffer, so they flex about 10⁻⁵ as much as the member; ignored where a plate runs along the member (it is cut at the plate's nodes instead); truss bars have none.

## 5. Known limits (0.4.4)

- Rigid floors: every height above the lowest is a floor (nodes of a sloping roof at many heights are not tied); a floor is tied by penalty links, accurate to about 10⁻⁷; it does not stiffen plates' membranes beyond the ties at frame nodes; 3D frames only.

## 5. Known limits (0.4.3)

- Tension-only / compression-only: static solves only (large-deflection steps, natural frequencies, buckling and dynamics use every member both ways); judged by a member's mean axial force; a frame left with nothing to resist a push is reported as a mechanism. Settlement: at nodes with supports, in held translations (not rotations), static solves only (left out of large-deflection steps). 3D second order: P-delta with small rotations (no arc length, no large rotations in 3D); plates add no geometric stiffness; hinged pieces' released rotations recovered with the first-order condensation.

## 5. Known limits (0.4.2)

- Dynamics: plane and 3D frames with plates (not solids); linear; one dynamic step on its own (no static loads with it: add by hand); a spectrum's results are unsigned peaks of each quantity separately (no balance, no concurrent forces); one direction of shaking at a time (no 100/30 combination); Rayleigh damping only (from the first two modes); a time history reports the record of one node and the whole model at one time (the time of the largest movement), not an envelope over time; masses lumped at nodes for plates, consistent for members; mass moves with translations only (no rotary inertia of point masses).

## 5. Known limits (0.4.1)

- Plates: thin (Kirchhoff, no transverse shear deformation), elastic, flat triangles (curved shells as facets); one plate per plane region (the last solid shape round a triangle's centre decides its thickness); plates on different planes join only where their meshes share nodes (an edge both have, meshed the same); beams inside a plate (not on its edge) join where its mesh nodes happen to lie on them; no temperature loads, buckling, point loads on a plate face away from a node, or line supports along a plate other than its edges; moving a plate (Move) on a Side or Plan plane is not handled. Nodal stresses averaged from the triangles (contours a little blotchy on coarse meshes).

## 5a. Known limits (0.4.0)

- Large deflection: plane frames only (3D frames, plates and solids later); elastic only (no yielding); loads keep their directions (no follower pressure); member loads go to the pieces' ends (exact as the pieces get shorter); shear deformation left out (Euler–Bernoulli); linear and large-deflection steps cannot be mixed in one sequence; the pieces of the first step are used for all. Arc length on a mixed vector of movements and rotations (unscaled). The diagrams are drawn along the undeformed members. Load control picks the stable branch at a bifurcation only with an imperfection or a side load to start it off (a perfect straight column stays straight, as it should).

- DXF: ASCII only (not binary DXF); blocks (INSERT), splines and ellipses are left out; the drawing's layers are not used to tell part from holes (nesting is). Large 3D solids (about 90 000 unknowns) take 8 to 12 s depending on the machine's load; faster needs a multilevel partitioner or another solver.

- 3D heat: steady only (no transient); no thermal stress in 3D solids yet (temperatures are not passed to a 3D stress solve, and uniform temperature changes are left out with a warning). 3D frequencies: consistent masses, the lowest 30 at most; no point masses. Mode search on large solids is slow (several seconds above 20 000 unknowns).

- Frames: combinations are solved one at a time (a few ms each for frames); the envelope is over combinations, or over cases when there are none; no plates or response spectrum yet; P-delta for plane frames only (0.4.0). A 3D hinge releases both bending moments (not one at a time). The tables' J leaves out the root fillets (a little below the tables). Load cases apply to every analysis; combinations to structural ones (not heat).

- 3D solids: the solve of large ones (about 90 000 unknowns) takes over 10 s here; the AMD ordering fills in a lot in 3D. No point supports or springs on solids; no thermal loads, pin loads or natural frequencies yet. The cutting plane shows values interpolated linearly inside each element (on corner nodes).

- Sketch tools: corners are rounded or bevelled only between two straight edges; offsets need arcs that meet their neighbours smoothly; a combined result whose arcs came from flattened pieces has its arc ends where the exact curves meet (to about 1e-9 of the part); no canvas picking of corners (by the dialog or the corner table).


- 3D frames: buckling by bending only (no flexural-torsional or lateral-torsional buckling: no warping stiffness); utilisation leaves out the shear stress of twisting; an angle bends about its leg axes (the product of inertia is left out); a channel's shear centre is not modelled.

- Buckling: frames and trusses only, in their own plane; linear (eigenvalue) buckling of a perfect frame; no lateral-torsional buckling or design-code checks.

- Natural frequencies: the six lowest only (up to 30 through the engine), no point masses yet, no damping or forced response.

- Axisymmetric loads are round the axis only (no bending of a shaft by a side load: that needs Fourier modes or 3D).

- Temperatures for thermal stress come from steady conduction only (no transient heating).

- Not yet tried on a clean Windows PC by a person (0.1.5).
- Stresses at the corner where a fixed edge meets a free edge, or at a sharp inside corner, keep rising with mesh refinement (a singularity); the help says so, the app does not mark them.
- NAFEMS LE1's error is flat between the first two meshes (0.24 %) before falling to 0.09 %.
- A pin load on an edge split in two (a corner added in it) is shared half and half, each half bearing on its own; on a circle hole it is one edge, so this rarely matters.
- No copy and paste of geometry.
- Overlapping solid shapes join only when meshed (0.1.2); the canvas draws them overlapping.
